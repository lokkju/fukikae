# fukikae

fukikae automatically dubs foreign-language TV into English. The pipeline extracts audio, separates speech from the background, transcribes, diarizes and builds a registry of recurring characters, then translates, speaks each line with the character's voice card, fits it to its slot, mixes and muxes a dubbed episode.

## Commands

```bash
uv sync                      # install
uv run pytest                # tests (gpu and media tests are skipped by default)
uv run ruff check && uv run ruff format --check
uv run pyright
mise -C . exec -- ffprobe -version   # ffmpeg comes from mise, not the host
```

## Stages and artifacts

`fukikae run <workspace> <episode>` runs, in order: extract, separate, language, asr, diarize, names, characters, tag (when `[tag] enabled`), translate, tts, timefit and mix. `fukikae run <workspace> E1 E2 ...` takes several episodes (file paths or SxxEyy codes) and runs them in order, with each episode's timefit and mix (CPU only) running beside the next episode's stages, so two Fairy Tail episodes took 45 minutes instead of 58 (D26); the GPU lock keeps every GPU step one at a time. It reports every episode's result at the end and exits 1 if any failed; an episode is not started after an earlier one failed. Each stage writes under `<workspace>/.fukikae/episodes/<episode>/`:

- `04c-names/names.json`: whether the episode's subtitles name the speakers well enough to use (`[names] use = "auto"`, the default: an ASS track whose Name column names at least half the dialogue lines with two or more names, no name on over 80% of them, not the style names, and lining up with the speech), the track chosen and every one tried. When they do, `turns.json` and `clusters.npz` beside it split each diarize cluster into one `<cluster>~<name>` sub-cluster per name plus an unnamed remainder, cutting the few turns that hold two names; nothing is re-embedded, so the stage runs on the CPU in seconds. `[names] use = "off"` reads no subtitles.
- `06-translate/cues.<target>.json`: the lines to dub (translated on the ASR path, the subtitle text on the subtitle path). With `[translate] source = "auto"` (every profile) the subtitle path reads target-language subtitles when there are dialogue subtitles: a `[subs] file` or `track`, a sidecar, or the embedded dialogue track; a track titled forced, or signs or songs unless the title also says full, dialogue or complete, or too sparse for dialogue (under 3 cues a minute, or 40% of the densest unflagged track, that rate capped at 30 a minute), is skipped, a forced flag is not believed on a track that carries every line (8 cues a minute or more), and a plain track wins over CC/SDH (ruling CD25).
- `07-tts/<cue>.wav` and `plan.json`: one render per spoken cue, silence trimmed, with every pause inside the line longer than `[tts] max_pause_ms` (200) cut down to it from its middle (D33: IndexTTS2 stops at commas, vocative ones too, and copies gaps from the Japanese emotion reference; up to 570 ms on Fairy Tail E12). A line with an ellipsis ("..." or "…") keeps up to `max_pause_ms_ellipsis` (500); `max_pause_ms = 0` turns the cap off. The text and subtitles are unchanged. The cap is a post-pass on the cached render, so changing it never renders again, and the rate fit measures the capped line. A backend that takes a speaking rate (Kokoro, for an operator's kept stock card) is rate-fitted up to `[timefit] max_speedup`; the cloning backends take no rate, so a clone line too long for its slot is left to timefit's time-stretch.
- `08-timefit/<cue>.wav` and `report.json`: each render placed in its slot. What the rate fit left is time-stretched (ffmpeg `atempo`, pitch kept; rate and stretch together never exceed `max_speedup`), then up to `borrow_silence_ms` of silence is borrowed after the slot and before it. A line that still doesn't fit plays whole past its slot and is listed under `over`; nothing is cut. `summary` counts stretched, borrowed and over lines.
- `09-mix/dub.<target>.wav`, `dub.<target>.ass` and `report.json`: the dub track and its subtitles (`Speaker: line`). The original vocals are removed over each dubbed line's whole slot and wherever its audio plays, and kept elsewhere; a line that is not dubbed (errored, untranslated, skipped) keeps its voice even where a dubbed slot overlaps it, and over songs and laughter the original vocals stay untouched. Songs come from a theme-song detector on the separation stems (openings and endings: loud music with near-constant, pitched vocals in the first 6 or last 4 minutes; every candidate window's scores are in the report), subtitle cues the filter marked as songs, and the tag stage's singing spans; a translated lyric inside a song is not placed, and `dropped` says which source caught it. The dub's level follows the original dialogue's, and the mix's EBU R128 integrated loudness the original mix's, by one linear gain and a true-peak limiter at -1.5 dBTP (headroom for the AAC encode), so the loudness range is unchanged (`loudness` in the report holds integrated loudness, range and true peak of the original, the mix before and after, and the dub track in the mkv after encoding).
- `out/<episode>.fukikae.mkv`: the video copied, the dub as the first and default audio track, every original audio track after it, the dub's ASS subtitles first and default, the original subtitle tracks after them (neither default nor forced), language tags set.

`[timefit]` takes `max_speedup` (1.25), `max_slowdown` (0.9; nothing is slowed today) and `borrow_silence_ms` (400). `[mix]` takes `keep_original_vocals` (true), `duck_db` (unset: removed; e.g. -18 keeps a quiet bleed of the original voice under the dub), `fade_ms` (40), `match_dialogue` (true), `protect_bgm` (false), `detect_songs` (true) and `true_peak_db` (-1.5, the limiter's ceiling); `[mix.songs]` holds the detector's thresholds and window lengths (`fukikae.songs.SongSettings`; `scripts/song_scores.py` scores episodes with them).

Two deliberate departures from the foundation spec (ruling CD11): the tag stage's BGM spans do not keep the original vocals by default, because SenseVoice marks BGM under ordinary dialogue (on Fairy Tail E11, 10 dialogue lines sat in BGM spans outside any song, which would have played the dub over the untouched Japanese line); and the original vocals are cleared over a dubbed line's whole slot, not only where the dub plays, because a dub shorter than its slot would otherwise let the original line resume mid-sentence.

To listen, open `out/<episode>.fukikae.mkv` in any player (mpv switches audio with `#` and subtitles with `j`), or play `09-mix/dub.<target>.wav` against `00-extract/audio.wav`.

## Watching a run

Every stage `fukikae run` runs logs a start line, with the stage's usual duration on earlier runs when there is one, and a finish line with its time. The long stages count their work: tts its cues, translate its cues, ASR the seconds of audio decoded (faster-whisper) or its chunks (Qwen3-ASR), SenseVoice the seconds of audio it has tagged and then its turns, emotion2vec its turns. tts counts only the cues that need a render: cues already in the render cache are logged once (`tts S01E12: 200 cues cached`) and left out of the rate and ETA, and a tts run that was mostly cached doesn't go into the ETA history. Separation, diarization, embedding and the mix are single calls, so they show only their elapsed time and the ETA from earlier runs, which keep moving while the call runs. On a terminal (not `TERM=dumb`) the counts are a single line at the bottom of stderr, redrawn as time passes and cleared for each log line; in a log (no TTY) each stage writes a line at most every 30 s, counted or not:

```
2026-11-11 09:31:12,410 [INFO] tts S01E12: started (about 14 min on earlier runs)
2026-11-11 09:31:43,385 [INFO] tts S01E12: 9/325 cues, 3.4 s/cue, 31 s elapsed, ETA 18 min
```

A job waiting for the GPU lock says who holds it, at once and every minute: `waiting for GPU 0, held by pid 81234 (tts indextts2) for 4 min 10 s`.

Each run keeps its own file, `.fukikae/runs/<host>-<pid>.json`, which it rewrites (atomically) every couple of seconds and at each stage boundary. From another shell, `fukikae status <workspace>` reads them all and shows every live run, then the most recent run that ended:

```
2 runs in progress

run b6920f53c6ca, pid 2480371, started 2026-11-11 09:17:38 (32 min ago)
episodes: S01E11 tail, S01E12 head
head: tts S01E12: 120/325 cues, 3.4 s/cue, 6 min 53 s elapsed, ETA 12 min
tail: mix S01E11: 41 s elapsed, ETA 1 min 10 s (earlier runs)
GPU 0 (head): held by this run (tts indextts2)
last log: tts: 3 cards checked
updated 2 s ago

run 51d07c2e9a14, pid 2481902, started 2026-11-11 09:44:10 (6 min ago)
episodes: S02E01 head
head: asr S02E01 (transcribe): 600/1420 s audio, 20.0 s audio/s, 1 min 05 s elapsed, ETA 41 s
updated 1 s ago

last finished run: finished at 2026-11-11 08:12:40; episodes: S01E10 done
```

`head` is the thread that runs the GPU stages and `tail` the one running the previous episode's timefit and mix in a multi-episode run (D26). `--json` prints the runs shown as `{"runs": [...]}`, each file with `live` added. When a run's pid is gone the command says so: `no run in progress (last run died at 2026-11-11 09:52:04; episodes: S01E12 head)`. A run on another host (a workspace on shared storage) can't have its pid checked, so it is live while its file keeps being updated, and stopped once the file is two minutes old: `running on render-box (pid not checkable here), updated 3 s ago`. On Linux a reused pid is told from the run by the process's start time in `/proc`; elsewhere a dead run whose pid was reused reads as live. The command deletes the files of dead runs older than a day, keeping the most recent one. ETAs before a stage's first count come from `.fukikae/stage-history.json` (the last 20 runs of each stage: duration, and count where the stage counts; runs sharing a workspace take a file lock to add to it), or failing that from the durations in the episodes' manifests.

## Subtitle speaker names

Crunchyroll-style ASS tracks name the speaker on every line, and diarization merges similar voices (young and female anime voices most of all), so when the names are usable they decide who said what (D27). The names stage (`04c-names/`, above) splits each diarize cluster into one sub-cluster per name before the characters stage, and the rest of the pipeline reads those sub-clusters wherever it read a cluster.

- **Who wins.** For the speech a name covers, the name wins over acoustics, and every disagreement is reported, never acted on. A locked actor or character (`registry lock`), a name the operator set (`registry rename`) and an alias the operator set (`registry alias`) win over names.
- **Mapping names to characters.** A key (the folded name: `NATSU`, `Natsu` and `ＮＡＴＳＵ` are one key) goes to the character that answers to it; else, for a role label (`TOWNSPEOPLE A`) or under 5 s of named speech, to an extra; else to an unnamed, uncurated character whose cluster is 60% that name (renamed, keeping its id and cards); else to a new character. Names are never matched fuzzily: a rare key one edit from a common one (`NABARU` 3 lines beside `NEBARU` 18 in the same episode) is listed as a suggestion with its `registry alias` command.
- **Voices.** Clone references come from a character's name-confirmed turns when it has any, else from its own acoustic speech; a turn with 10% or more of it under someone else's name is dropped either way. An automatic clone card with a reference 10% or more under another character's names is re-picked at the next tts run; a card someone chose, locked or imported is only flagged (`voices list`, `names show`).
- **`[names]`** (a settings table with no backend, the same in every profile): `use` (`auto`, `on` to skip the share and dominance tests, `off`), `file` (an operator ASS file, `{episode}` replaced, relative to the source's folder), `track` (a subtitle stream index), `min_coverage` (0.5), `turn_share` (0.6), `min_new_seconds` (5), `align` (true), and for evaluation only `holdout_episodes`, `hide_fraction` and `hide_seed`.
- **`fukikae names scan <ws> [episodes...]`** runs only the detection test, read-only (embedded tracks are cached under `.fukikae/cache/subtitles/`), and prints each episode's chosen track, named share, keys, verdict and alias suggestions.
- **`fukikae names show <ws> <episode>`** prints the verdict, track, offset and coverage, each key's seconds and sub-clusters with the character it went to and how (`known`, `renamed`, `created`, `extra`, `lock`, `ambiguous`; the characters stage records this in `05-characters/names-report.json`), cut turns, unvoiced lines, voice disagreements lowest cosine first, alias suggestions, a `registry merge` hint for a nameless character that kept a cluster's remainder while its named speech went to one named character, stale or flagged clone cards, and characters still holding exemplars from episodes run without names.
- **The manual fallback**, for releases without names (licensor and Bilibili subtitles, SRT): `registry split <ws> --episode E --cluster C` (a sub-cluster `C~name` too), `registry rename`, `registry lock`, and `voices clone <ws> <character> --clip <file>`. Since `registry split` can't separate two voices inside one diarize cluster, `[names] file` can point at an ASS file the operator writes or borrows from another release; a partial file is fine, only named lines act.
- **Migrating a series.** Every episode's characters stage reruns once after upgrading; without usable names it reproduces its assignments byte for byte. To move a series onto names: `fukikae run <ws> <every episode, in order> --from names`, then for the anime profile `fukikae registry resolve <ws> --episodes ...`. Earlier episodes keep their mixed exemplars until they are rerun, and `names show` says which.
- **`fukikae eval names <ws> --tune E,... [--holdout E,...] [--ceiling E,...] [--gt-file ...] [--force] [--set NAME] [--tag ...]`** scores cue attribution and reference purity. Cue attribution: the subtitle path's cue speakers against the ground-truth names, each character standing for its modal name over the tuning episodes' speech (turns under hidden lines left out). Several characters can share one modal name, and each of them then scores as that name, so an over-split registry isn't penalised there; the characters per name are printed beside the accuracy and recorded in the row. Reference purity: the auto-picked clone references in ground-truth seconds, `pure` under lines of the character's modal name, `foreign` under another name's lines, `unverifiable` under no line; purity is pure / (pure + foreign), with the unverifiable share beside it, per split and per character. Ground-truth lines are aligned to the speech as `eval identity` aligns them (`--force` scores a track that doesn't line up). Splits: `tune` (labelled a ceiling where the tuning episodes' names were used, since they are graded on the names they read), `holdout` (protocol A; it refuses an episode whose `names.json` or assignments show it read names), `ceiling` (`--ceiling`: protocol A's ceiling arm, scored as held out without that check, labelled as graded on its own input) and `hidden` (protocol B: only the cues a withheld line of the cue's own name overlaps, as the subtitle path tests them; the names and `use = "off"` arms must draw them from the same track, and each row's detail records `hide_fraction` and `hide_seed`). An episode whose names weren't used has its cues attributed to turns moved by the alignment shift. It also refuses an episode whose assignments are older than its names (run the characters stage first). `fukikae eval identity` takes `--ceiling` the same way. Rows go to the results ledger with `stage = "names"`.

## Voices: cards, cloning by default, packs

Every recurring character speaks with a voice card per target language: a small TOML file in the workspace's card store (`.fukikae/cards/<id>.toml`, the id a hash of what the card sounds like), with its reference clips and fingerprint beside it. The registry records which card each character has, with a numbered history.

**Automatic cards.** The first time a character needs a voice in an episode and has no card for the target language, the tts stage gives it one, `auto` and unlocked, and logs a line to `07-tts/assignments.log`:

- With `[tts] clone_backend` set (every profile sets `indextts2`, the operator's pick after a blind listening test, D24), a clone card of that backend. Its reference clips are the character's cleanest turns on the separated vocals across the episodes the workspace has processed: no overlap with another speaker, 3 to 12 s each, an ASR transcript that isn't implausibly sparse for its length, outside theme songs and tagged singing, ranked by signal-to-noise with turns that emotion2vec scores non-neutral last (up to 3 clips, 20 s). Before the 3 s check, a character's turns at most 0.6 s apart with nobody else's turn between them are joined (up to 12 s), so speech the diarizer or the subtitle names cut into fragments still counts. Non-neutral turns are taken only to reach the clone floor of 2 clips and 10 s. When the 3-clip limit stops short of a floor the episode's turns can reach, the best 3 clips that reach it are used instead, giving up a neutral clip only when no other combination gets there. When a chosen clip is scored non-neutral, the log and `voices show` say `references may carry emotion: <labels>`.
- A character whose best picks fall short of the floor (3 s or more of gate-passing speech, but one clip or under 10 s) still gets that clone, and it is flagged for review in the tts run's `run.json` flags, `voices list` and `names show`, naming the shortfall: give it a better clip with `voices clone`. An automatic clone card under the floor is re-picked at the next tts run when the current rules find a better pick (D32), which is how cards made before the floor existed get more clips.
- A character with under 3 s of gate-passing speech is cloned anyway, from its longest turns up to 15 s (IndexTTS2's prompt limit) with the reference gates set aside, theme songs and speech the subtitle names give someone else still left out, and flagged `thin reference: <seconds> s from <n> turns`: listen to it in `fukikae studio`, or replace it with `voices clone`. A character that speaks but holds no turn of its own in any episode is cloned the same way from its own lines' source spans, and flagged; when one of its episodes was skipped because its assignments are older than its names, the flag names the `--from characters` rerun.
- Extras (unrecurring speakers) get an episode-local clone of their own turns in that episode, under the same tiers, and each line with no speaker cluster at all a clone of its own source span (one flag per episode counts them; a line whose span is silence or music is cloned anyway and flagged). Extras cards live in the tts stage key, not the registry.
- fukikae never renders a stock voice it chose (D41). An automatic stock card from before (made under 6 s of speech, or as an `auto-fallback` stand-in while cloning couldn't run) is re-picked as a clone at the next tts run, noted `re-picked: stock voices are retired (D41)`; a stock card someone assigned, imported or locked is kept and flagged on every run.
- An automatic card never replaces a card that exists, except automatic cards themselves: a clone card whose references the subtitle names now give another character (10% or more of a reference) is re-picked at the next tts run, as is one under the clone floor when a better pick exists, one whose references the reference gates refuse, and an automatic stock card. A card someone chose, locked or imported is never replaced, only flagged (`voices list`, `names show`, one line per card with every reason). To start over, assign a card by hand.
- Clone cards are made, and clone renders that aren't in the cache rendered, only when the clone backend can run here: its worker environment set up from its current lock and its device present (IndexTTS2 needs a GPU). For a render, the backend checked is the one that renders the card, so a machine with only the engine `[tts] engines` names for it (`indextts2_trt`) set up renders. When a card has to be made or re-picked, or a clone card's render isn't cached, and that backend can't run (or no `[tts] clone_backend` is set), the tts stage stops before any model loads, naming the backend and `fukikae backends setup <backend>`. Extras cards are rebuilt from `[tts] clone_backend` on every run, so an episode with extras needs it set even when every render is cached; an episode with only characters, whose cards all exist and whose clone renders are cached, renders without it. A device typo in `[tts.<backend>]` is an error.

`[tts] backend` no longer chooses any card: the profiles name none, and a workspace that still sets it gets a warning once per run (the key stays accepted, and names the backend `[tts]`'s own options go to). Qwen3-TTS and CosyVoice 3 also passed the listening test; a character can be given either with `voices clone --backend`. Kokoro stays as a TTS backend for two things only: rendering a stock card an operator assigned, imported or locked before D41 (kept and flagged), and `eval tts` baselines.

**Changing a voice.** `fukikae voices <command> <workspace> <character> ...`, where a character is its registry id or name:

- `list`, `show`, `history`: what each character has, the current card (`references may carry emotion` and the clone mode included), the assignment rows.
- `assign <card>`: a card id from the store, or one character's card from a pack (`<pack>:<name>`); locked unless `--no-lock`. A stock voice (`stock:kokoro:<voice>`) is refused, as is a stock card in a pack import (D41).
- `clone --clip <wav> --transcript TEXT [--backend NAME]`: a clone from a clip you cut yourself, 3 to 30 s of the character alone (checked like any card the stage renders), for a character whose automatic references are wrong (a cluster that merged two voices, say); assigned locked.
- `edit` (a copy of the card in `$EDITOR`, assigned locked), `derive --to <lang>` (the same voice for another target language), `revert [--to N]`, `lock`, `unlock`. `restock` is retired with stock voices: the next tts run re-picks every automatic stock card as a clone.
- `verify [--record]`: renders a fixed test script and compares its speaker embedding with the card's fingerprint.

Locked cards and cards an operator chose are never replaced by automatic assignment or by an import without `--replace`.

**Packs.** `fukikae voices export <workspace> -o <pack>[.tar.zst]` writes the named characters' current cards, clips and fingerprints to a pack, first recording a fingerprint for any card without one (one render on its backend; listed when that backend can't run here or the render fails, or with `--no-record`; refused with `--require-fingerprints`, ruling CD26) (names and external ids, never registry ids); `fukikae voices import <workspace> <pack>` assigns them to another workspace's characters by external id, then by name, as `import:<title>`, unlocked, and verifies each fingerprinted card. A pack is untrusted input: it is read without extraction, every member checked against the layout and size limits, cards re-parsed and their ids recomputed, and a fingerprint can only name an allowlisted embedder that is already on disk, so a pack can never cause a download or load code.

**Models and privacy.** `fukikae backends setup <name>` is the only command that downloads: worker environments (`uv sync --locked`) and every pinned model, IndexTTS2's included (`fukikae backends setup indextts2` before the first run). Runs load models from the local cache only, with pyannote's, Hugging Face's and onnxruntime's telemetry turned off for the whole process and for every worker (ruling CD24). Ten older backends (ASR, alignment, diarization, dialogue separation and four embedders) still fetch an unpinned model on a cache miss; pinning them is open work.

## Cloning backends

The tag models (SenseVoice, emotion2vec), the managed translator's GGUF and Kokoro run inside fukikae and load their pinned files from the local Hugging Face cache only; fetch them once with `fukikae backends setup sensevoice` (and `emotion2vec`, `openai_compat`; `--workspace <dir>` fetches the models that workspace's config names). Kokoro renders no voice fukikae picks (D41), only a stock card an operator kept from before and `eval tts` baselines, so `fukikae backends setup kokoro` is needed only for those. A run never downloads them, and fukikae turns off pyannote's, Hugging Face's and onnxruntime's telemetry for the whole process.

The cloning backends run out of process, each in an environment of its own, so their pinned dependencies never meet fukikae's: `workers/<name>/` is a separate uv project (its own Python and lock) and its environment lives in `.venvs/<name>`. fukikae starts the backend as a worker process speaking JSON lines (`fukikae.backends.tts.remote`), on one GPU only (`CUDA_VISIBLE_DEVICES`), and stops and reaps it when the stage ends. `fukikae doctor` lists each worker environment and the command that sets it up. IndexTTS2 (`[tts] clone_backend = "indextts2"` in every profile, or a card on it) needs Python 3.11, which uv downloads:

```bash
fukikae backends setup indextts2
```

That runs `uv sync --locked` into `.venvs/indextts2`, records the lock's sha256 there (a worker whose environment came from another lock refuses to start and names this command), then downloads the model and its four auxiliary models at their pinned revisions into the Hugging Face cache. It is the only step that downloads: the worker runs offline, with an allowlisted environment that carries none of the secrets in fukikae's own. The environment takes about 8 GB, the models about 7 GB. IndexTTS2 clones from a card's reference clips and takes the source line's audio as a separate emotion reference, scaled by the card's `emotion_strength`; it has no duration control.

CosyVoice 3 (`cosyvoice3`, Fun-CosyVoice3-0.5B) needs Python 3.10:

```bash
fukikae backends setup cosyvoice3
```

CosyVoice and its Matcha-TTS submodule ship no package, so the setup step also downloads each one's code at a pinned commit into `.venvs/cosyvoice3/fukikae-code`, keeps only the package directory and refuses it unless its files hash to the pinned digest; the worker checks that digest again every time it loads. The environment takes about 5.5 GB, the model about 5 GB, and a run about 4 GB of VRAM. It clones from a card's reference clips: `[tts.cosyvoice3] mode = "auto"` (the default) gives the model the clips' transcripts when they are in the target language (`zero_shot`) and only their sound when they aren't (`cross_lingual`, CosyVoice's own advice for a foreign-language prompt); `mode` can force either. Every line is neutral: it takes no delivery direction, emotion audio, emotion vector or duration target (CosyVoice's instruct mode is not offered until an A/B shows it helps), and the card's `rate` is CosyVoice's speed. CosyVoice's own control tags (`[laughter]`, `[breath]`, `<strong>` and its phoneme tags) are stripped from the text before it reaches the model. It runs without a text normalizer: CosyVoice's wetext fetches its grammars unpinned at every start, so it isn't installed, and CosyVoice still spells out English numbers.

Qwen3-TTS (`qwen3_tts`, Qwen3-TTS-12Hz-1.7B-Base) runs on Python 3.12:

```bash
fukikae backends setup qwen3_tts
```

The `qwen-tts` package is pinned by version (and by hash in `workers/qwen3tts/uv.lock`) and checked again at every load; the model is pinned by revision. The environment takes about 7 GB, the model about 4.3 GB. It clones from a card's reference clips: `[tts.qwen3_tts] mode = "auto"` (the default) gives the model the clips' transcripts and speech codes (`icl`) when every clip has a transcript, and only the speaker embedding (`xvector`) otherwise; `mode` can force either. It takes no delivery input (no direction, emotion audio, emotion vector, speed or duration target), and the card's `seed`, `temperature` and `top_p` are its only voice keys. Only the clone model is integrated; Qwen3-TTS's VoiceDesign model (`source = "design"`) is not.

### IndexTTS2 on TensorRT (opt-in)

`indextts2_trt` renders IndexTTS2 cards through upstream's TensorRT backend: the same weights and prompt handling, about 2.2 times as fast a line on a 3090, and voices measurably further from the characters (every similarity measure lower in both samples, `docs/results/2026-11-09-character-cards.md`, "D28 lever 2"). It is opt-in and off in every profile (D30); the PyTorch worker stays the default. A workspace turns it on with

```toml
[tts]
engines = { indextts2 = "indextts2_trt" }
```

The cards stay `indextts2` cards and nothing about them changes. The renders carry the engine's name and identity (the worker project's digest and the engines' build id) in their cache keys, so turning it on or off re-renders those lines and never mixes the two engines' audio. An engine makes no cards, so `indextts2_trt` is refused as `[tts] clone_backend`, as `[tts] backend` and as `voices clone --backend`, with a message pointing here. It reads its own table, `[tts.indextts2_trt]`, whose one option is `fp32`: a list of the plain TensorRT engines to build without fp16 (`speech_semantic_encoder`, `semantic_codec`, `campplus`, `speaker_perceiver_conditioner`, `emotion_perceiver_conditioner`, `latent_projector`, `length_regulator`, `dit`, `bigvgan`; the GPT stays fp16 on TensorRT-LLM). Without it every engine is fp16. Each `fp32` set gets its own engines directory, `<digest>-fp32-<hash>`, beside the default one and its own identity in the cache keys, so fp16 and fp32 renders never share an entry; build it with `fukikae backends setup indextts2_trt --workspace <workspace>`, which reads that workspace's table. The fp32 engines are larger and need more VRAM: the seven conditioners in fp32 take 4.7 GB of engines and 18.6 GB of VRAM (more than the 16.5 GB the backend reserves, which is the fp16 figure), and all nine don't fit beside TensorRT-LLM on a 24 GB card. They didn't close the likeness gap (`docs/results/2026-11-09-character-cards.md`, "D30"), so leave `fp32` unset. `[tts.indextts2]` options such as `fp16` and `tf32` don't carry over. `voices verify` and the fingerprint `voices export` records render on the card's own backend, PyTorch IndexTTS2, whatever `[tts] engines` says.

The worker needs OpenMPI, which the host doesn't carry, so it runs in a local Docker image, never pushed. Setup needs Docker and the NVIDIA Container Toolkit (`nvidia` in `docker info`'s runtimes):

```bash
fukikae backends setup indextts2_trt            # --force rebuilds engines that are current
```

That checks docker, its NVIDIA runtime and the disk first, then builds the image from `workers/indextts2_trt` (about 24 GB under docker's root; 14 minutes the first time, 2 to 4 after a lock change), fetches the pinned models into the Hugging Face cache, and builds the engines for GPU 0 under its lock into `~/.cache/fukikae/indextts2_trt/<digest>/`: about 26 minutes on a 3090 (9 exporting ONNX graphs, 1 converting the GPT, 16 building), 3.1 GB of engines plus about 5 GB of intermediates while they build. The engines are built for the GPU's compute capability; setup skips them when engines for this digest and this capability exist. A run takes 15.1 GiB of VRAM, against about 9.3 GB for the PyTorch worker, because TensorRT-LLM reserves its KV cache up front.

At run time the container gets one GPU, no network, the caller's uid, no capabilities, a read-only image, the models and engines read-only and one writable directory for the line's files; it is removed before the GPU lock is released, and the next load removes any left by a run that was killed. With `[tts] engines` naming `indextts2_trt` and Docker missing or down, or the image or engines not built, the tts stage stops with an error naming the setup command. It never falls back to the PyTorch worker, which would mix two engines' renders in one episode without saying so; to render on PyTorch, take the line out of `[tts] engines`.

Each TTS backend takes its options and its device from its own table, `[tts.<backend>]` (for example `[tts.indextts2] fp16 = true`). Kokoro runs on the CPU and the cloning backends on the GPU unless that table's `device` says otherwise; the old stage-wide `[tts] device` applies only to a backend that declares no default. IndexTTS2 refuses the CPU (fp32, about 9 GB of RAM, minutes per line) unless `force_cpu = true` is in `[tts.indextts2]`. The profiles set `[tts.indextts2] tf32 = true` (TF32 matmuls, which speed up the diffusion decoder: about 12% faster a line, within the eval gate); like every backend option it is part of each render's cache key and of the tts stage key.

## Profiles

A workspace's `fukikae.toml` picks a tested set of identity settings with `profile = "anime" | "live-action"`; without it the `default` profile applies (WeSpeaker embeddings, the centroid matcher at 0.6, Demucs, pyannote). Precedence, highest first: the workspace's `[x.pair.P]`, its `[x.lang.L]`, its `[x]`, then the profile's `[x.pair.P]`, `[x.lang.L]` and `[x]`, then the built-in fallbacks; a layer that names a different backend drops the options of the layers below it. A workspace `[embed]` table that sets options must name its `backend`. Measured results and held-out verdicts are in `docs/results/2026-11-07-identity-profiles.md` for anime and default, and in `docs/results/2026-11-09-live-action-ground-truth.md` for live-action (superseding the 2026-11-07 doc's live-action section).

- `anime`: tuned on Fairy Tail. The best setting is the joint matcher with episode cannot-link on at 0.6. Joint resolves a whole season at once, so it runs as `fukikae registry resolve <workspace> --episodes S01E01,S01E02,...` after the episodes are diarized, reading the profile's `[resolve]` table (`matcher`, `threshold`, matcher options such as `episode_cannot_link`); flags on the command override it, and a workspace's own `[resolve]` overrides the profile's. The per-episode characters stage keeps centroid.
- `live-action`: picked on live-action ground truth in English, German, Spanish, Japanese and Korean (`docs/results/2026-11-09-live-action-ground-truth.md`). It lowers pyannote's `clustering_threshold` from 0.6 to 0.5, which splits the Spanish dub's leads, and keeps the centroid matcher at 0.6 in the characters stage. Its `[resolve]` is joint at 0.6 with `episode_cannot_link = true`, the joint setting that tied with centroid. The German dub's two leads stay on one id at every setting measured.

Anime fails the 90% held-out bar on Fairy Tail E14 (ja 0.55, dub 0.60), and so does default on the same tracks (ja 0.55, dub 0.60). Live-action, held out once after its pick: it passes BBT S01E03 in English and Spanish (4 of 4, no merge) and Bazinga's The Office S01E05, and fails BBT S01E03 in German (0.80, Leonard and Sheldon on one id), Bazinga's TBBT S01E05 (0.67: Howard at 0.52, and Raj, who has no id from the tuning episodes) and Grid E05 (0.80, no merge). Kaze, Kaoru's held-out E11 fails on both profiles (0.50: 直美 lands on りん's id and りん keeps 0.76). Details: `docs/results/2026-11-09-live-action-ground-truth.md`.

The live-action profile's `clustering_threshold = 0.5` is part of the diarize stage's cache key, so an existing live-action workspace re-runs diarize, and the stages after it, on its next run.

The default embedder is now WeSpeaker (it was ECAPA), so a workspace with no `profile` re-runs diarize on its next run.

Changing the embedder (a different `[embed]` backend or model, a profile switch, or the WeSpeaker default above for a workspace with no `profile`) leaves the registry's exemplars and centroids in the old embedding space, and matching refuses them. Run `fukikae registry reembed <workspace>` first (`--dry-run` reports counts per dimension): it embeds every exemplar again from its episode's existing diarize spans with the configured embedder, recomputes the centroids and drops projections of the old dimension, keeping names, locks and voices. Then re-run the pipeline. A locked actor's centroid, and that of an actor with no exemplars left, is never recomputed from exemplars: it is kept as stored when the dimension is unchanged, and otherwise carried into the new space by a ridge linear map fit on the old and new exemplar pairs, an approximation, since two embedders' spaces are not linearly related, so check a mapped actor's next matches or re-lock it after a run (reembed refuses, naming those actors, when there are fewer than 2 x the old dimension pairs; unlock them first and lock again afterwards). Run it before diarize re-runs: the exemplars' cluster labels refer to the existing diarization, and reembed refuses an episode whose diarization has changed.

## Upgrade notes

Stage cache keys and the clip-set and identity embedding caches now include the resolved model of each backend (the model file or name, plus the Hugging Face revision when known), so a changed default model re-runs the stage instead of reusing old output. The first run after this change re-runs separate, language (when it detects), asr and diarize once in each existing workspace, and `eval identity` recomputes its cached embeddings. The Hugging Face revision in a key is read from the local cache, so a fresh cache (or a `refs/main` that moved) changes a stage's key once, and the first episode run afterwards re-runs that stage once. The CAM++ default is now the zh_en 3D-Speaker model; a workspace using `campplus` needs `fukikae registry reembed` as for any embedder change.

## Ground-truth tools

Commands that build or check speaker ground truth for `eval identity` (`docs/results/2026-11-09-live-action-ground-truth.md` shows them in use):

- `fukikae eval subalign`: finds the offset and linear drift from one subtitle file to a reference subtitle file (by text) or to a workspace's detected speech (`--speech`), and writes the file retimed; it refuses a capture with a cut or a jump unless `--force`.
- `fukikae eval transcript-align`: names the cues of a timed subtitle file from an untimed, speaker-named transcript (bigbangtrans pages), written as named ASS for `--gt ass`.
- `fukikae eval colour-map`: maps each caption colour to a character from the named lines of all the given episodes and writes `labels-colours.yaml`, which the `colour` reader uses.
- `fukikae eval bazinga-labels`: turns Bazinga's word-level speaker annotations into named ASS, keeping confident words only, with `--time-scale` for audio slowed to film speed.
- `fukikae eval embedders`: scores embedders by nearest-centroid accuracy on a folder-per-character clip set and writes one ledger row per embedder.

## Results ledger

`fukikae eval asr`, `fukikae eval identity`, `fukikae eval names` and `fukikae eval embedders` append one row per run and split (tune or holdout; ceiling for `eval names` and `eval identity --ceiling`; hidden for `eval names`) to a single user-level JSONL file, `$XDG_DATA_HOME/fukikae/results.jsonl` (`~/.local/share/fukikae/results.jsonl` by default); `FUKIKAE_RESULTS` or `--ledger` points them elsewhere, `--no-ledger` skips the write, and `--set` and `--tag` name the test set and the run. Each row records the backend, model and revision, a hash of the options, the language, the genre and title from the workspace's `[series]` table (`title` defaults to the workspace directory's name, `genre` to the profile's name; `tmdb`, `tvdb` and `anidb` are accepted for later use), the episodes, the metrics and fukikae's commit. `fukikae eval report` renders the ledger as a matrix, one row per stage, test set, split and configuration and one column per `language/genre`, with `--stage`, `--backend`, `--set`, `--split` and `--tag` filters, `--metric` for a metric other than the headline one, `--format md`, and `--export PATH` to write the filtered rows as JSONL. A line it can't read (a write cut short, a row from a newer schema) is skipped with a warning naming the line.

An explicit `[series] title` also names the series that `fukikae registry adapt` stores the LDA projection under (the older top-level `series = "Name"` still works); without one the key is `default`, never the directory-name default of the title. When a workspace that has a projection stored under `default` gains a title, the characters stage keeps using that projection and warns, naming both keys, until `registry adapt` fits one under the title.

Heavy backends are optional extras: `fukikae[demucs]`, `[whisper]`, `[qwen]`, `[pyannote]`, `[sherpa]`, `[wavlm]`, `[dialogue]`.
